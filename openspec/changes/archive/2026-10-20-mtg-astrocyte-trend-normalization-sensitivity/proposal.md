# Proposal

## Why

The feature-selection change (`mtg-astrocyte-feature-selection-sensitivity`, archived 2026-10-19) showed that the first latent axis of the primary analysis survives every feature-content setting: removing the ribosomal, mitochondrial and hemoglobin genes, replacing half of the RNA features by a trend-ratio ranking and replacing every ATAC peak by a mean-accessibility ranking left `pls_0` correlated −0.81 / −0.70 with the ribosomal fraction and −0.73 / −0.64 with the RNA TMM factor; only the label-blind residualization removed it, at the price of a held-out CV AUROC at chance (0.41 / 0.48), and the adoption check of the candidate failed in `cohort_50`. A label-free check on 2026-10-20 (scratch, not a step; stage and sex were not read) located the axis one step earlier, in the normalization: in both cohorts `pls_0` correlates −0.94 with each donor's mean z-score over the selected RNA block, and that shift is an abundance-dependent tilt that one TMM factor per donor cannot remove. The per-donor slope of the normalized log-ratio (M, the donor's log2-CPM minus the per-feature mean over donors) against abundance (A) correlates 0.90 with `pls_0`; the median M of the rarest twentieth of genes correlates −0.89 with it and that of the most abundant twentieth +0.78; the tilt explains 83 % of the across-donor variance of the selected set's shift (SD 0.37 → 0.21 log2 units once it is removed); and library size plays no role (ρ 0.27). The selection draws from the rare end (kept median mean log2-CPM 0.77 against 3.13 for all abundance-passing genes), so the tilt enters the block as one shared level shift. ATAC tilts the other way (slope ρ −0.77 with `pls_0`; weak peaks relatively higher in high-`pls_0` donors, the pattern of libraries with poorer signal-to-noise, consistent with the axis's −0.77 correlation with TSS enrichment), and removing its trend drops the selected set's correlation with `pls_0` from 0.50 to 0.15. A per-donor trend normalization (cyclic loess of the log2-CPM against the average donor, limma's `normalizeCyclicLoess`; csaw's loess offsets for ATAC) is the established correction for this: it uses no label and no technical covariate, so it fits the export's rules, and it costs one smooth curve per donor rather than degrees of freedom per feature. It assumes that most features do not change at any abundance level, a stronger assumption than TMM's, so it is a predeclared sensitivity variant with the existing adoption rule, not a change of the primary export.

## What Changes

- **A normalization setting of the export**, `normalization`, per omic, with the values `none` (the primary's) and `cyclic_loess`: after log2-CPM and before the residualization and the selection step, each donor's log2-CPM over the abundance-passing features is corrected by a loess curve of its M against A, fitted against the per-feature mean over donors and iterated as in limma's fast cyclic loess, with limma's default span rule. The fitted settings (span per omic, iterations, reference) and a label-free tilt accounting per donor (the slope of M on A and the median M of the lowest and highest abundance twentieth, before and after) are recorded; the per-donor tilt table is written beside the blocks as `normalization_trend.csv`. The primary export's output stays byte-identical (`normalization = none`).
- **Two registry entries** in `PREPROCESSING_VARIANTS`:
  - `trend_normalized`: cyclic loess for both omics; every other setting the primary's. The adoption rule is evaluated for it.
  - `revised_candidate_trend`: the archived candidate's four settings plus cyclic loess, in the primary's order (families removed → abundance → TMM → log2-CPM → cyclic loess → residualized → ranked by trend ratio for RNA and mean accessibility for ATAC). The adoption rule is evaluated for it.
- **A predeclared precedence rule** beside `ADOPTION_RULE`, written here before any run: if `trend_normalized` meets the rule in both cohorts, it is the candidate a later revised-primary change proposes, whatever `revised_candidate_trend` gives (fewer assumptions: no gene removed, no degrees of freedom spent, no technical covariate read); if only `revised_candidate_trend` meets it, that one is; if neither does, the variants stay sensitivity analyses. The rule, its variables and the threshold are unchanged.
- **An R cross-check**, `scripts/check_cyclic_loess_limma.R`, comparing the numpy cyclic loess of the export's debug files with `limma::normalizeCyclicLoess` on the same log2-CPM, in the style of `scripts/check_tmm_edger.R`.
- **The comparison gains the normalization accounting** (per omic: the rule, span, iterations, the per-donor tilt statistics before and after as quantiles over donors, the largest absolute correction) beside the accounting it already writes; one report, `reports/motco-astrocyte-trend-normalization-<date>.md`, reads both variants as sensitivity, attributes the change of the first axis's technical correlations to the normalization through `axis-diagnostics` on the variant roots, and states the outcome of the adoption and precedence rules.

Out of scope: rerunning or modifying the primary export, analysis, rank sweep or the six archived variants (read and checked, as before); a per-gene-class normalization (the lncRNA-versus-coding offset at matched abundance that the check found beside the tilt, ρ −0.72 with `pls_0`, is recorded as a finding for a later proposal, not corrected here); other `N` values; the forced-rank sweep on a variant; any MOTCO change (the covariate-aware latent construction is proposed in the MOTCO repository separately); the revised-primary change itself.

### Modified Capabilities
- `motco-preprocessing-sensitivity`: the registry grows from six to eight entries; a variant may change the normalization setting per omic; the variant export pipeline gains the normalization step between log2-CPM and the residualization, cross-checked against limma; the feature accounting and the comparison carry the normalization accounting; the adoption check is evaluated for every variant the registry marks, with a precedence rule between candidates; the report requirements extend to the new variants and their limitations.

## Impact

- `src/sead_sexdiff/data.py`: `preprocess_block` and `_export_cohort` take a `normalization` keyword (per omic) defaulting to `none`, so `export` output stays byte-identical; a `cyclic_loess` helper (statsmodels' `lowess`, the same Cleveland algorithm as R's `lowess`, which limma's `loessFit` calls); `NORMALIZATION_RULES`, `LOESS_ITERATIONS`, `LOESS_SPAN_RULE` constants; `PREPROCESSING_VARIANTS` extended with the two entries; `ADOPTION_PRECEDENCE` constant; `_feature_accounting`, `render_preprocessing_sensitivity` and the manifests made normalization-aware; `_write_debug` also writes the normalized log2-CPM.
- `src/sead_sexdiff/cli.py`: `--variant` accepts the two new names (choices come from the registry); no new flags.
- `scripts/check_cyclic_loess_limma.R`: new; `flake.nix` unchanged (limma 3.68.4 is present through `rPackages.edgeR`; csaw is not, and is not needed because the cross-check target is limma's function, which csaw's offsets also call).
- `pyproject.toml` / `uv.lock`: `statsmodels` becomes a direct dependency (`uv add`) if it is not one already (it is in the lockfile as a transitive dependency).
- `README.md`, `AGENTS.md`: the two variants, the normalization step and its constants, the precedence rule, the reading rules.
- `reports/`: one new committed report. `results/` stays gitignored.
- Compute: two variants × two cohorts, one export (the ATAC loess on about 129,000 peaks × 80 donors × 3 iterations is the slow part, minutes) and one CV analysis (about two minutes) each, on the workstation that holds the kept outputs (`nixos-home`).
- Scientific choices introduced here (flag and ask before changing): the normalization step's position (after log2-CPM, before residualization and selection), fitting set (all abundance-passing features of the block, as TMM), reference (the per-feature mean over donors, limma's fast method), iterations (3) and span rule (limma's default adaptive span); the two registry entries; the adoption check on `trend_normalized`; `ADOPTION_PRECEDENCE`; `n_jobs = 1` for reported results; the rule that the primary analysis stays primary and that no rule reads p-values.
